PHOTOSHOP BASICS · BEGINNER

What Is a PSD File? A Beginner’s Guide

A PSD file is the native project format associated with Adobe Photoshop. Unlike a common flattened image such as a JPEG, a PSD can preserve a project’s layers, masks, text, and other editable components when those elements are present in the file.

Why do layers matter?

Layers let an editor work on one part of a composition without necessarily changing every other part. For example, a title, a person, and a background may be stored separately in a well-organized project. This can make revisions easier, although the actual editability depends on how the file was created.

Does every PSD contain editable text?

No. A PSD may contain text as editable type layers, or the letters may simply be pixels in an image layer. Converting a flattened image into layers does not automatically recover the original fonts, exact wording, or design decisions.

IMAGE EDITING · FUNDAMENTALS

Flat Images vs. Layered Images

A flat image stores the visible composition as a single raster image. A layered project stores separate visual components on different layers, making it possible to hide, move, or adjust individual components when they have been separated properly.

When is a layered file useful?

Layered files are helpful when a design needs revisions, such as changing a background, repositioning a subject, or adjusting a title. The quality of the result depends on the available source material and how carefully the layers are prepared.

What are the limitations?

If an object is removed from a flat image, the pixels behind it may never have existed in the source. A tool must estimate or reconstruct those missing areas, so traces, blur, or mismatched texture can remain. Manual review is often necessary for professional work.

AI & DESIGN · EXPLAINER

What AI Image Segmentation Can—and Can’t—Do

Image segmentation is the process of identifying regions or objects in an image. AI-assisted systems can help outline a person, object, or other visual region, which can then be used to create a mask or separate layer.

Why do results vary?

Fine hair, transparent materials, low contrast, overlapping people, and small details can make boundaries difficult to detect. A mask may include background pixels or miss parts of the subject, particularly when the image is small or visually complex.

How can you improve a result?

Use a clear source image when possible, inspect the mask at full size, and refine edges manually when accuracy matters. Treat AI output as a useful starting point rather than a guarantee of a perfect cutout.
