JPG vs PNG vs PSD: What's the Difference?

Discover the differences between JPG, PNG, and PSD, including image quality, transparency, file size, and the ability to edit individual elements in a design.

Published October 20, 2026 · LayerAI

Why Do Image File Formats Matter?

Image formats determine how visual information is stored and how you can use a file. Some formats are designed for sharing photographs, others are useful for transparent graphics, and some preserve the editing information needed for complex designs.

JPG, PNG, and PSD are common formats, but they are not interchangeable in every situation. Choosing the right one can make your workflow easier and help you avoid unnecessary quality loss or editing problems.

What Is JPG?

JPG, also known as JPEG, is a widely supported image format commonly used for photographs and images published online. It uses lossy compression, which reduces file size by discarding some image information.

Advantages of JPG

  • Usually produces relatively small image files.
  • Works on almost all devices and websites.
  • Suitable for photographs and everyday image sharing.
  • Useful when storage space and upload speed matter.

Limitations of JPG

  • Does not support transparent backgrounds.
  • Repeated editing and saving can introduce compression artifacts.
  • Does not preserve separate Photoshop layers.

Best for: photographs, website images, and situations where a smaller file size is important.

What Is PNG?

PNG is an image format that uses lossless compression. It preserves the stored image data and supports transparency, making it useful for logos, icons, interface graphics, and cutout objects.

Advantages of PNG

  • Supports transparent backgrounds.
  • Preserves image details without lossy compression.
  • Works well for logos, screenshots, and graphics containing text.
  • Useful for objects cut out from their original backgrounds.

Limitations of PNG

  • Photographs may have larger file sizes than JPG files.
  • A standard PNG does not preserve separate Photoshop layers.
  • Text inside a flattened PNG is not automatically editable.

Best for: transparent images, logos, icons, and detailed graphics that need clean edges.

What Is PSD?

PSD is the native document format associated with Adobe Photoshop. It can preserve separate layers and other editing information, allowing you to work on individual parts of a design.

Advantages of PSD

  • Can preserve separate objects and image layers.
  • Can retain editable text layers when they are present.
  • Supports layer masks and many advanced editing features.
  • Makes it easier to revise a design without rebuilding everything.

Limitations of PSD

  • Files can be much larger than JPG or PNG images.
  • Some applications may not support every Photoshop feature.
  • Saving a flat image as PSD does not automatically separate its contents.

Best for: layered design projects, Photoshop editing, and work that may need future revisions.

JPG vs PNG vs PSD: Main Differences

The biggest difference is how each format stores image information. JPG is optimized for compact photographs, PNG is useful for lossless images and transparency, and PSD can preserve an editable document structure.

  • JPG: usually small files, but no transparency or layers.
  • PNG: supports transparency and lossless compression, but not separate layers in a standard file.
  • PSD: can store layers and editing information, but may require more storage space.

Can You Convert JPG or PNG into PSD?

Yes. You can open a JPG or PNG in Photoshop and save it as a PSD document. However, the original image will normally remain a single flat layer unless you separate its elements yourself or use tools that help you do so.

If you want independent layers for people, products, text, and backgrounds, you must extract or recreate those elements. AI tools may help identify objects or text, but results depend on the image and may require manual correction.

Which Image Format Should You Choose?

  • Choose JPG for photographs and sharing images when a smaller file is useful.
  • Choose PNG for transparent graphics, logos, screenshots, and sharp visual elements.
  • Choose PSD when you need to preserve separate layers and continue editing a design.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is PNG better than JPG?

Neither format is always better. PNG is useful for transparency and lossless graphics, while JPG is often better for photographs when smaller file sizes are important.

Is PSD better quality than PNG?

They serve different purposes. PSD can preserve layers and editing information, while PNG is commonly used to display or share a finished image. The visible quality depends on the image data and export settings.

Can a JPG contain editable text layers?

No. Text in a standard JPG is stored as part of the image pixels. To edit it independently, you generally need to remove or cover the original text and create a new text layer.

Does converting PNG to PSD create separate layers?

No. Simply saving a PNG as PSD does not automatically separate the objects. You must create or extract the individual layers separately.

Conclusion

JPG, PNG, and PSD each have a useful role in image editing. JPG is convenient for photographs, PNG is ideal for many transparent graphics, and PSD is designed to preserve editable layers and other project information.

Understanding these differences helps you choose the right format for your project and plan your editing workflow.
