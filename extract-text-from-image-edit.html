How to Extract Text from an Image and Edit It

Have you ever wanted to copy words from a picture, poster, screenshot, or scanned document? You can use Optical Character Recognition (OCR) to detect text in an image and turn it into editable text.

In this guide, you will learn how OCR works, how to extract text from an image, and how to recreate that text when editing a design.

What Is OCR?

OCR stands for Optical Character Recognition. It is a technology that analyzes an image and attempts to recognize the letters and words inside it.

For example, OCR can help you copy text from a screenshot, read a scanned page, or recover words from a poster when you no longer have the original editable file.

Important: OCR usually extracts the words as text. It does not automatically restore the original font, color, position, or design. You may need to recreate the text in an image editor.

Step 1: Choose a Clear Image

Start with the clearest version of your image. High-resolution images usually give OCR tools a better chance of recognizing the words correctly.

  • Use an image with sharp, readable letters.
  • Avoid blurry or heavily compressed images when possible.
  • Make sure the text is not hidden behind other objects.
  • Crop the image to focus on the text if that helps.

Step 2: Use an OCR Tool

Open an OCR tool that supports your language and image format. Depending on the tool, you may be able to upload an image, scan it, or select text directly from a screenshot.

A typical OCR workflow looks like this:

  1. Open the OCR tool.
  2. Upload your image or select the relevant area.
  3. Choose the correct language if the tool offers that option.
  4. Run text recognition.
  5. Copy the extracted text into a document or text editor.

Some tools offer free features, while others limit usage or require payment for certain functions. Check the current terms before using a service regularly.

Step 3: Correct the Extracted Text

OCR is not always perfect. It can confuse similar-looking characters, miss punctuation, or incorrectly recognize letters in decorative fonts.

Before using the extracted text, check for:

  • Misspelled words and missing letters.
  • Incorrect punctuation and numbers.
  • Words that were joined together or split apart.
  • Incorrect line breaks and reading order.
  • Language-specific characters and accents.

If the image contains Arabic, French, English, or another language, make sure the tool supports that language. Handwritten and highly stylized text can be more difficult to recognize.

Step 4: Make the Text Editable in Your Design

If you want to change the text on a poster or graphic, extracting the words is only part of the process. You will usually need to open the image in an editor and create a new text layer.

  1. Open the image in your preferred image editor.
  2. Identify the original text you want to replace.
  3. Remove or cover the old text carefully, where appropriate.
  4. Create a new text layer and enter the corrected words.
  5. Adjust the font, size, color, alignment, and position.
  6. Save an editable project file if your editor supports one.

The original letters are usually part of the image pixels, not separate text objects. That means simply opening a JPG or PNG will not normally make its existing text editable.

Why Doesn't OCR Recognize All the Text?

Several things can make text recognition difficult:

  • Low resolution: small letters may be too blurry.
  • Complex backgrounds: text can blend into nearby objects.
  • Decorative fonts: unusual letter shapes can confuse OCR.
  • Curved or rotated text: the tool may read it incorrectly.
  • Overlapping elements: graphics can hide parts of letters.
  • Multiple columns: the reading order may be incorrect.

When recognition fails, try a higher-quality image, crop the text area, or test another OCR tool. Always proofread the result.

Can You Extract Text and Keep the Original Design?

Extracting the words and preserving the original design are two different tasks. OCR focuses on recognizing text, while recreating the design requires matching the appearance and placement of the original letters.

For a simple poster, you may be able to recreate the text manually. For a complex design, you might need to adjust several text layers and carefully restore the background behind the original lettering.

Privacy tip: Before uploading an image to an online service, consider whether it contains private documents, personal information, or confidential material. Check the service's privacy policy and use a trusted tool.

Final Thoughts

OCR can save time when you need to extract words from screenshots, posters, scanned documents, and other images. For the best results, start with a clear image, select a tool that supports your language, and carefully correct the extracted text.

Remember that recognizing the text does not automatically restore the original editable design. If you want to change the words inside a graphic, you may need to recreate them as separate text layers.
